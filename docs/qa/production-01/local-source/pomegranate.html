How to Tell If a Pomegranate Is Ripe

Choose a pomegranate that feels heavy for its size and has intact skin. Slight angles can offer another clue. Pomegranates do not continue ripening after harvest.

HOW TO PICK

01

Same size. Compare the weight.

Pick up two before you choose.

Compare fruits of similar size, one in each hand. Prefer the one that feels heavier for its size, then check its rind. This is a practical selection clue, not a guarantee of sweetness.

Look for
A substantial, heavy feel for its size.
Watch out for
Comparing a large fruit with a small one; size alone tells you little.

02

Inspect the rind all the way around

Turn the fruit to check the sides and base. Choose intact skin; avoid buying fruit with open cracks, cuts, major bruises, or decay. A small surface scuff alone is less concerning.

Inspect the surface detail

03

Slight angles are a supporting clue

A mature pomegranate may have gently flattened sides. Shape varies, so a round fruit is not automatically a bad choice. Use this clue alongside weight and rind condition.

Inspect the outline detail

04

Different colors. Different varieties.

Redder does not always mean better.

Rind color depends on the cultivar. Pale pink and deep red can both be normal. These examples show color variation, not a ripening sequence. Pomegranates will not ripen further on your counter.

Look for
Good condition and a heavy feel, whatever its normal rind color.
Watch out for
Picking the darkest red fruit without checking it, or waiting for it to ripen at home.

FAQ

How do you choose a ripe pomegranate?

Look for a fruit that feels heavy for its size, has intact skin, and may be somewhat angular rather than perfectly round.

Does a pomegranate ripen after you buy it?

No. Pomegranates do not continue ripening after harvest, so choose one in good condition at the store.

Should a ripe pomegranate feel heavy?

For two similarly sized fruits, the heavier one is generally the better pick.

Are cracks in a pomegranate a bad sign?

Meaningful cracks, cuts, significant bruising, and decay are reasons to choose another fruit.

How should a whole pomegranate be stored?

Keep whole fruit cool and handle it gently to protect the intact rind.
